# Lecture Notes

## Debrief + Toulmin's Argument Structure 
In terms of our Debrief: 
* Our device was our ground. 
* Teardown breakdown was *primary* research, and Research was *secondary research* by finding some information from a source. 

## Arguing with Integrity 
The concept of Arguing with Integrity is having the capability to interact with and learn from ideas that you may not personally agree with. 
### Steel-Manning
The counterpart of the Strawman Argument, where one considers the strongest possible position of the opponent. This method may help you either further develop your argument, or changing your mind. 

That is, you embrace their ideas! Explore ideas which are presented. 


## Arranging Arguments 
So far, we've been working with Toulmin's Argument structure *raw*, independent of their presentation (i.e. the logic behind what we put into how we arrange our information for presentation) 

In terms of Praxis: 

* We work with what we have from the Data.
	* aka Inductive Arrangement
	  Follows closely basically with all of the research that we needed to do. 
* But we *frame* with the claim when presenting it. 
	* aka. Deductive Argument 
	  Writing for reading, i.e. for the reader to be able to understand what we're discussing. 

What this means is that we work from the *claim* towards the *evidence and justification* when presenting it to our reader. 

Recall that in terms of *developing* our claim, we often work from *data*, then into the significance of the data and conclusion. 

The reason why we have it in this arrangement, is due to readers simply potentially not needing all of the data (as well as the research) that we needed to build up to *our main idea*. We want to present and start with our *new idea*, then work backwards to show where it originates from our data. 

We want to present our argument *differently* than the inductive work that went into developing our argument. 

**i.e. TRY TO FRAME WITH THE CLAIM OFTEN**. 

### Toulmin's Extended
* We normally don't verbally articulate the ground. 
* We can start with a qualifier to decrease the scope of our argument.
