# Lecture Notes
## Stakeholder Expectations
Stakeholders (individuals impacting or is impacted by a project) have a direct stake within a project with their own *Needs*, *Goals*, and *Objectives*. These are informed by **Requirements** and **Evaluation Criteria**.

> Needs, Goals and Objectives (NGOs) are part of Stakeholder Expectations, 
> > These can also be negotiated, and updated throughout the process. 
> 
> 
> **Requirements** and **Evaluation Criteria** would be then part of the design requirements, informed by the NGOs in the process. 

> [!theorem] Requirements
> What the design **must do** to meet the need (defined by stakeholders). Basically quantified objectives. 
> > Aspect/Property, with a unit and limit. 

> [!theorem] Evaluation Criteria
> To describe a potentially better solution. 
> > Aspect/Property, with a unit and utility gradient (i.e. what is being optimized) 
> > An example of a utility gradient would be minimizing mass. 

### NGOs
> [!theorem] Needs
> A clear statement of the root problem to solve / opportunity to meet. 

> [!theorem] Goals
> What the system/project must do meet the need / what does success for the project look like? 

> [!theorem] Objectives
> Specific, *measurable* targets to allow us to evaluate if goals are being met or not. 

To make an objective measurable, the property should be some *standardized* unit in some way (ideally). Note however that real world standards differ often from the ideal standardized unit. 

You *always* need to be aware with theoretical ideals, and practical reality. You need to find a *balance* of both—being workable for you as well as acceptable in the profession.
