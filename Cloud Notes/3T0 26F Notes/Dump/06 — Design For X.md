# Lecture Notes
Let's extend our previous backpack example by inserting the new goal: 

> Goal 2: The backpack should be able to last for at least 4 years of normal use. 

Note that we can now consider this goal under a "Design for X" framework, such as Design for Durability or Design for Reliability. The question is now— how do we define these terms? 

## Design for X (DfX) 
DfX Principles generally help us define **Specific** objectives based on ***common goals***. These are often codified in Handbooks for their corresponding design principle (e.g. Design for Reliability). 

These results will *often* be incredibly domain-specific, i.e. these design principles will have these design principles often in high specificity to a certain concept. 

> [!quote] Example: Design for eXcellence's Design for Reliability
> DfX's Design for Reliability principles has overall objectives that include
> * Ensuring that the device has environmental protection mechanisms. 
> * Simplifying the construction of the object, 
> * Ensuring that the parts that go into manufacturing the object are proven parts (i.e. tested) 
> * Ensuring that there exists redundant mechanisms and safety measures in place
> * Ensuring that there exists options to repair the object.
> * Do the components have generous safety factors / factors that are likely to affect their lifespan? 
> 
> There exists a good amount of principles from DfX for DfReliability; although some principles apply more depending on the contexts of the device being discussed.
