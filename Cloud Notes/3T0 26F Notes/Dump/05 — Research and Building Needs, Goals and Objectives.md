# Lecture Notes
## Intro to Research
In general, we want to use research of *formal patents* of standards in order to develop the proper evidence/claim we need for Toulmin's Argument Structure. We want to use a proper formal source of standards (e.g. ISO's standards). These formal standards often have more quantifiable properties over a mere statement (e.g. more descriptors on it's performance and function) 

For instance, taking a ballpen example, we can formally claim that it follows *the ISO standards* of what a ballpen should be. From the standard, we can also derive some proper testing parameters that we could use to determine some values that we can test against. 

### Updating Toulmin's Structure 
From a different ground / source of claim (e.g. changing it to a Standard-based approach), we can change the entire backing structure of Toulmin's Argument Structure: 
* We can use test results as evidence, and use their results as justification to show the parameters that we wanted to display. 
	* Note that as evidence changes, interpretation must change (which also plays into the definition of the claim). 
* As our qualifiers, we could then limit our test parameters in order to standardize it to a general regime. 
* And from here, with a properly defined set of limits/standards/data, we can define a counterpoint. 

## Research 
Research *could* be defined under two standards: 
1. Finding *existing* sources for facts, information, standards, and definitions pertaining to the concept we are researching. (Secondary) 
2. Discovering something *new* though our direct action (Primary) 
	1. e.g. experimentation. 

### Secondary Research
Secondary Research is research done indirectly, i.e. through external sources. This is generally defined to "looking at things that already exist". 
1. Finding pre-existing literature which explains the concept. 
2. Finding existing designs with the same goals / principles. 
3. Finding standards (or similar media) or design. 
You generally want to perform Secondary Research first before performing Primary Research: Find out what's out there first before trying to write something again; since you may be unintentionally reinventing something unnecessarily. 
### Primary Research
Primary Research is research done directly: 
1. Interacting with experts / stakeholders,
2. Doing tests
	1. Either of design aspects via a model
	2. Testing the underlying principles for work
3. Discovering new phenomena
Though, generally, you only want to do primary research if you absolutely have to, i.e. no information within secondary research was sufficient for our purposes. 

## Building NGOs 
Building NGOs (Needs, Goals and Objectives) starts with first finding an *Opportunity*. An opportunity could simply be potential improvements based off data, or maybe simply updating some designs. We want to generally follow this as: 
1. Finding an Opportunity / Establishing an Opportunity
2. Identifying Stakeholder 
3. Eliciting Stakeholder Expectations[^1]
4. Synthesizing and Document

[^1]: That is, trying to figure out what they actually fully expect based on our opportunities. 
Refresh yourself on [[03 — The Requirements Framework]] in terms of the exact definitions of Needs, Goals and Objectives. 
### Identifying Opportunities
We can find identities though the same Frame - Diverge - Converge framework that we've been using all this time throughout Praxis: 
* We frame what we're looking to improve / work towards, 
* We diverge to find potential opportunities to do our goal,
* We converge to find which things we should update. 

### Application
For instance, we can retake our backpack example in terms of NGO: 

> Need: We need to find a backpack suitable for most EngSci students. 

From here, we can do some *primary research* or secondary research to identify what Stakeholders want in our Need. They may give us expectations (such as a backpack carrying alot). Such expectations can be formalized into a set of goals and objectives which we can quantify. 

> Goal: The backpack should be able to carry everything that students need for an average day. 

From this goal, we can find our Objectives via research / quantifying our values: 

> Objective 1.1: The bag should have these measurements (??)

> Objective 1.2: The bag should be able to carry up to (?)Kg

For Objective 1.2: We could work towards finding some research to specify a carrying capacity for a backpack. From here we could also solve for the proper ergonomic load of a backpack through our research. **Always do secondary research before investing resources in primary research!**

Note that often while doing secondary research, more terms may arise, such as "ergonomics". Should this be something that we also define one of our objectives on? We could do further research into this description in the long run. Note that we can also use these terms researched for further clarifying / specifying our research queries.
